Sr. SEO Analyst working with D2C, wellness, and lifestyle brands. The day-to-day is technical SEO, content strategy, and growth — site audits, core update recoveries, indexability fixes, content architecture, and the shopping/discovery side of e-commerce.

§ 02 — Selected Work

Three brands. Three different kinds of stuck.

RecentD2C Men's Bottomwear Lead, SEO & Growth

A brand mid-pivot, betting on ads to do what positioning should have done.

The brand was shifting from custom-tailored to ready-to-wear — a category jump that confuses both Google and customers. The two product lines were competing against each other in search, so the brand kept showing up for the wrong queries. The team was spending heavily on paid ads to mask the problem instead of fixing it.

The fix was structural: rebuild the site's SEO setup so the two product lines stopped competing for the same searches, then build out content around how people actually shop for bottomwear — outfit pairings, fit guides, fabric comparisons. On the commercial side, the brand's products were optimized for ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini — because product discovery is quietly moving there.

RecentLegacy Ayurvedic Wellness Lead, Google Core Update Recovery

A trust crisis disguised as a Google penalty — and what it took to fix.

The brand lost 61% of its organic traffic in four days after a Google Core Update. On the surface it looked like a search algorithm problem. Underneath, the real issue was credibility — the site was making absolute health claims it couldn't back up, the content was AI-spun, and a recent domain change had stripped away years of trust signals.

The approach was a brand audit, not just an SEO fix. Content that overpromised got pulled, educational pages were rewritten with real medical sourcing, and actual clinical experts (with credentials and bylines) moved to the center of the brand's storytelling. Google rewards what real people trust — once the trust came back, so did the traffic.

RecentEnterprise Luggage & D2C Bag Brands Lead, Technical SEO

A brand paying ad rent because its own website was hiding from Google.

The brand's regional store pages — its biggest discovery asset — were stranded on a part of the site that Google barely registered. Around 90 high-intent product pages were live but effectively invisible in search. The team had been compensating by spending on paid ads, treating the symptom instead of the cause.

The fix was structural. The site's SEO setup got restructured so the right pages would actually show up when people searched. Regional stores were brought into the main brand experience, and the technical scaffolding was cleaned up. The result: the brand stopped paying rent to Meta for traffic it should have been getting for free.
